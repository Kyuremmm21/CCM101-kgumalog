# Mission Reflection

Among the three major cloud platforms, Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most. I was surprised by how strong it is in Artificial Intelligence and Kubernetes. The tools they offer for machine learning and data analytics look modern and powerful. Even though AWS is the biggest and Azure is very good with Microsoft products, GCP felt more focused on future technologies like AI.

AWS, Azure, and GCP have many similarities. All of them provide virtual machines, object storage, networking, and identity management services. They also use the same basic idea of regions and availability zones to keep applications available even if one data center has a problem. Another common thing is the pay-as-you-go pricing model, which means you only pay for the resources you actually use. This makes cloud computing flexible for both small and large companies.

Business requirements play a very important role when choosing a cloud platform. A startup with limited budget will look for something affordable and easy to scale. A university that already uses Windows Server and Microsoft 365 will naturally prefer Azure because of better integration. An AI research company will benefit more from Google Cloud, while a global e-commerce business needs the wide infrastructure and reliability of AWS. The best cloud is not always the most popular one. It depends on what the company already uses and what it needs in the future.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me to think carefully before recommending a platform. I learned that technical features are important, but understanding the client’s situation is even more important. It is not enough to know the services. You also need to match them to real business needs.

After completing this mission, my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio became more organized. I now have clear research documents, comparison tables, and screenshots that show my progress. This laboratory helped me improve both my technical knowledge and my documentation skills.
